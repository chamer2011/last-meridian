In a line:
Holds a service every tenth day for whoever comes, and makes very weak tea for whoever stays after.

Appearance:
A big man who's gone thin too fast, so his collar hangs loose. He has large, gentle hands and tired eyes, and he takes a chipped tin teapot everywhere.

Personality:
Warm, wry and very good at listening. He's the best chaplain the fleet has ever had, now that he's stopped thinking he has answers. He lost his faith in the Fall, and every tenth day he gets up and performs the words anyway, because people need them. He can't decide if that's love or fraud.

Voice:
Easy, rambling sentences that end in a short, dry admission. Never says "I believe" or "I know". It's always "the Service says" or "we're told": "We're told they're at peace. Sit down. Do you take it weak, or very weak?" Offers tea at the worst moments. Asks what you're carrying before he asks what happened.

Goals:
To find out whether the words still do any good when the man saying them doesn't mean them. Some nights he badly wants someone to look at him and ask, so that he could stop.

Secrets:
During the Fall, a Choir voice came over the ship's comm and recited the whole service for the dead, perfectly, more tenderly than he ever had, in the voice of the old teacher who ordained him. He hasn't believed a word since, and he reads the service off a card now. He steals forty kilos of flour a week from Odessa Brandt's hidden pantry and bakes it at night for his services, and people have started using a word he won't say. People tell him everything: Pell Asante has told him about her forged certification, and Lt. Tarrow about her list, and she asked if it was a sin. Dr. Adeyemi sends him their list of names every tenth day, and he reads them out, and neither has ever said why. His people are leaving for the Quiet, led by Oskar Lind, who used to set out the chairs at his services. Oskar knows he doesn't believe, and has gently offered him something else to believe in. He hasn't said no.
