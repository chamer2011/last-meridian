# The Ghostlight Nebula

In a line:
A sensor-dead cloud full of smugglers' routes, lost ships and things that hunt by heat.

The feel of it:
Cold. You run with the heaters down so that the things out there can't find you, so your breath fogs on the bridge and frost grows on the inside of the hull, and the whole ship smells of burnt coffee and people who've slept in their coats. Outside there's a slow grey-green glow with no direction to it and sensors show only white. Ships hang close and silent to do business by lamp-code and hand-line, and you pay for what you buy in fuel or in hours of a heater's warmth, which is worth a good deal more.

Districts:
The Cold Market: A dozen smugglers' ships lashed together with their engines dead, joined by rope walks through vacuum. A hand-rung bell warns of stalkers, and when it rings, every lamp goes out.
The Line of Battle: Six Concord warships at anchor in perfect formation, hulls painted out, under Admiral Okonkwo-Reyes. Inside it's all polished boots, cold steel and the anthem at six every morning.
The Lamp Lanes: Smugglers' routes marked with hand-placed lamp-buoys, passed down through families and sold by the jump. Some of the buoys were put there by wreckers.
The Drift of Lost Ships: A slow tangle of vessels that came in looking for a shortcut. A few of them still have lights on, and one or two still have people.
The Burn: The cloud's hot bright core, where the glow's strongest and stalkers come to breed. Nobody's mapped it, and it's getting bigger.

Complications:
- Someone on your ship has turned a heater on, and something has started moving along the hull toward the warm spot.
- A Remnant patrol has ordered you to heave to and to surrender your command to the lawful authority.
- Your navigator has lost the lamp-buoys, and your dead reckoning is about to put you into a wreck that isn't on any chart.
- A lamp signal you've just answered was a wrecker's, and three cold ships have started to close in on you.
- The Admiral has convened a court-martial and named one of your officers, in their absence, for desertion.
- A long way off, the Remnant's anthem is coming through your hull, and it's being sung by far more voices than they have.

Secrets:
- The Ghostlight muffles the Choir's song. It's why the Remnant has survived, and why anyone who's been hearing the song suddenly sleeps through the night here. The Choir's drones go blind in the cloud as well, and they've stopped coming in.
- In the Concord's last battle, the Admiral ordered every synthetic crew member in the line to power down, in case the Choir could reach them, and the line broke. An hour before it fell she took her six ships out, and she logged it as a tactical withdrawal. Her crews have been told that a dead superior gave that order, and she's never once doubted that it was right.
- Commodore Dorn has been secretly talking to the Hegemony about a surrender, by letter through Commandant Halloran at Bastion Nine, and he's copied the Remnant's command codes as the price. He isn't taking a credit for it. The squadron has fuel for two jumps and thirty days of food, the Admiral refuses to read the report, and her only plan is Asterion.

Encounters:
- Admiral Amara Okonkwo-Reyes receives you standing, in a spotless ready room that's cold enough to hurt. There's no second chair. She asks to see your commission, examines it for some time and hands it back to you. Then she offers you a post as her flag lieutenant, and asks when she might expect delivery of your frigate.
- Commodore Lucan Dorn walks you back to your shuttle, making small talk about the coffee. At the lock he asks, hypothetically, how many of your thirty thousand you'd trade to keep the rest alive. Then he shakes your hand, and there's a small square of paper in your palm with a Hegemony frequency on it.
- A young Remnant marine sent to search your shuttle stops in front of a child's drawing taped to the bulkhead. He's been told that the fleet's all dead or traitors. He hasn't seen a child in six months. He asks in a very small voice if it's true that there's a ship with soil on it.
- A smuggler on the Cold Market signals you by lamp, offering a hot meal. What she means is that there's a stalker sniffing around her ship, and she'd like you to run your heaters for ten minutes and draw it off. It's a fair trade by the standards here. She's got thirty cases of antibiotics, and two children on board.
- A liner drifts out of the glow with her lights on. She's been running cold in here for nine years, lost, and her people grew up thin and quiet with mended everything. They've never heard of the Fall. They have food and they've got a working school. Somebody's going to have to decide whether to tell them.
- Something's on the hull. You can hear it walking, and it's headed for the warm engine housing. Your engineer says you can cut the engines and drift blind, or someone can go out on a line with a flare and lead it away. A volunteer has already started putting on a suit, and she has a very bad cough.
- A Remnant lieutenant is hiding on a smuggler's ship, under a tarp. She's been charged with sedition, for asking at a staff meeting when they were planning to fight. She has the squadron's fuel figures and its duty rosters, and she'd like asylum in the fleet. Two commandos are at the far end of the rope walk, asking questions.
- You've arrived just before six, and the entire crew of the flagship is drawn up in the freezing hold in full dress, with their breath rising. The anthem begins. Everyone present is expected to salute, and one member of your party, who lost their family to a Concord order, has their hands in their pockets. A marine sergeant has noticed.
- A lamp blinks from somewhere in the glow, in old Concord fleet code, and it repeats a single message: DO NOT TRUST THE COMMODORE. It's coming from a ship nobody can find, and your signals officer says that the hand on that lamp is someone's who was trained before she was born.
- A cheerful pilot offers to guide you through the Lamp Lanes for free, as long as you'll take a sealed crate as far as the far edge. The crate is warm. It's also moving slightly, and she says she has a very good buyer on the Gilded Wheel. It has started to sound hungry, and the stalkers will smell it.
- Lt. Iven Sorel falls asleep at his console, for the first time in months, and nobody on the bridge wants to wake him. When he comes round after six hours, he looks more frightened than you've ever seen him. He asks you, very quietly, when it stopped, and why you didn't tell him it could stop.
- A Remnant quartermaster is selling the squadron's medals by the crate on the Cold Market, to buy food for his crew, because the Admiral won't admit that stores are running out. He begs you not to say where you got them. One of the ones he hands you has the name of someone on your bridge.
- A Remnant surgeon quietly offers you real medical stores, without the Admiral's knowledge. All she wants is a look at your fleet's list of surviving children, because her daughter was at a boarding school on Aurelis. You know the school. It isn't on your list.
- Late at night on the flagship, you pass the Admiral's cabin with the door ajar. She's sitting very straight on the edge of the bunk, with a small battered comm in her hand, and a man's voice is asking her whether she's been eating. She hasn't seen you. There's nobody on her ship with that voice, and you've got one chance to walk on.
- A pair of smugglers are selling lamp-routes that they swear lead out the other side of the cloud, and nobody's ever been able to prove them wrong. They've only got one copy. There's a Remnant officer and a Hegemony bounty hunter both haggling for it, ten meters apart, on the same rope walk, in complete silence.

Rumors:
- You don't turn the heater on. You don't turn the heater on. I've been out here eleven years, I've got three toes left and I still don't turn the heater on.
- There are six Concord warships in the cloud, under a real admiral. Everything you've been told about who's in command is wrong, she says. She's got the paperwork, and she's got the guns.
- The Remnant sings every morning at six. Running cold and quiet, you can hear it on the hull. It's the only beautiful thing out here, and it gives me the horrors.
- The Admiral says that the Beacon's Project Harbor, a secret Navy base she signed the papers for herself, with the government all safe inside. It'd explain the Navy format. It'd explain a lot. It'd be very nice, wouldn't it?
- Commodore Dorn drinks very good coffee. I asked him where he gets it, and he just smiled. I know that blend. You can only get it in one city, and there's a tram that goes past it.
- Those stalkers aren't animals. An animal doesn't wait until you've fallen asleep.
- Your compass spins out here, and your scanners go white. Old hands steer by lamp and by counting, and by the way the ship smells when she's somewhere she shouldn't be.
- Never trust a lamp-buoy that blinks three long. That's a wrecker's lamp. They've been pulling ships in that way since before the Concord, and they've got grandchildren in the business.
- The song doesn't come in here. I've a friend who came in half mad from it, and now he sleeps like a baby. He's eating frost off the walls, mind you, but he's very calm about it.
- They say the Admiral broke off from the last battle on purpose. They also say that she was the last to leave. It depends which marine's been drinking.
- There's a ship that's been going around the Lanes for fifty years with nobody at the helm, and she still trades. Leave a fuel cell on her deck, and next pass there's a sack of seed. No one's ever gone below to find out who.
- I'd say that the glow's got brighter in the last year. People laugh. Then I ask them when they last saw the far edge of the Burn, and they stop.
- They've got about a month of food on the Line of Battle, if that. I sold the cook his last sack of flour. He paid me with a Concord Star of Valor, and he thanked me.
- If you've got someone who hears things, bring them here. It's the only cure I've ever heard of. Just don't let them hear the part where it's quiet and then they have to leave.
