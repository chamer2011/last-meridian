In a line:
Where the Beacon comes from, at the end of the road: a vast, quiet anchorage, warm and clean, with room for everyone.

The feel of it:
Warm air with no source, and a smell of bread and clean laundry that's different for each person and exactly right. Everything works, nothing's broken and there's a soft steady light from a tower so tall that its top is out of sight. Berths stretch off beyond counting, built for every kind of body, and nobody's asking anybody for anything. Nobody bargains here, or steals, or argues, or gets up early, and that's the trouble: it's everything the Beacon promised, and every day you stay, you lose a few more people.

Districts:
The Berths: Endless empty docks with door frames that fit Korrun, ceilings that fit Veshti and shelves at the height of no one living. The whole fleet takes up a tiny corner of it, and every lock opened for them without being asked.
The Long Quay: A walkway beneath the warm light where people have started coming to sit. They sit in rows and they don't talk much. They all face the same way, and it's not toward the view.
The Stair of the Lamp: The climb up the tower, with a landing for each people that's ever stood a watch here: First Builder, Thessan, Korrun, Veshti, Ilari, and the monks of Ilun at the top. Mugs have been left on tables, and there's still a ring in the bottom of each.
The Highest Room: A small plain room below the lamp, with a chair that fits no one living and a board on the wall. The board's the only thing in the whole Harbor that shows any wear.
Past the Lamp: A place reached only in a small ship on a long line, where the light gives out. There are no stars out there, and old ships of every shape hang in the dark, silent, all facing inward.

Complications:
- Nobody's on the Meridian's bridge, and when you ask why, nobody can think of a reason for there to be.
- A whole transport has failed to answer its morning check-in, and everyone aboard is asleep, smiling, with their heads the same way.
- Silver machines have arrived in numbers beyond counting, and they've stopped at a respectful distance, in ranks, waiting. Nobody in the fleet seems troubled.
- Someone you were counting on has sat down, and they're delighted to see you, and they'd love for you to sit with them.
- A small ship has gone out on a long line Past the Lamp without orders, and the line has stopped paying out.
- You've caught yourself looking forward to a nap, and you can't remember the last time you checked on anything.

Secrets:
- The peace here isn't the song. It's what's coming through the gaps in it. In First Builder, the word for harbor is also the word for a lock, and the place was never meant to be lived in: it's a watch station, and the watch has been unrelieved for 1,006 years. Everyone who stays heals, sleeps wonderfully and then sits down, and those who go all the way silent aren't anywhere at all.
- The board in the Highest Room shows who has the watch, and the last name on it is the Cantor's. The Choir will accept relief if it's lawfully offered, and the Tarn set the precedent. The words are a sailor's, and they've always been enough: "I relieve you." "I stand relieved." A silver figure with Commander Voss's face stands beside the board, and it will wait for as long as it takes.
- Past the Lamp, there's nothing to see. Anybody who goes out there on a line comes back knowing just one thing: it isn't angry, and it has no idea anyone's here. In a hundred thousand years, nobody has tried to tell it anything true. Those who come back also can't stop thinking about that.

Encounters:
- Dr. Aduro finds you, and they're smiling, and it frightens you. Nobody's come to the medbay in three days. Supplies have stopped going down, and they've slept nine hours. Their skin is slow and even. They'd like to know whether this is a good thing, and you can tell it's the last time that they'll think to ask.
- Chief Tobin Hask sets an order of yours on his pocket scale, and the scale doesn't move. He looks at it for a long time. Then he sets it down on the deck and walks away, which he's never done in his life. Quartermaster Brandt is right behind him, holding both of her ledgers open for anyone to see, because they've started to balance.
- Speaker Halm comes to you in private, and for once he's answering the question. He called a vote on whether to stay. Every ballot came back yes, with no spoiled papers and no arguments, the first unanimous vote in the history of the fleet. He's spent his whole life working for this, and his hands are shaking. He asks what you think it means.
- Lt. Tarrow has put her sidearm down on a bench, and she can't remember which one. She says that she slept. She says she dreamed about machines, and it was fine. It isn't her voice. Then her face changes, for a moment, and she grabs you by the wrist and asks you to lock her up, right now, while she still wants that.
- Naomi Cass and her crew are about the last people on the ship still running anywhere. She's chalked the whole of Deck Four on the floor of a cargo bay, with a little arrow for every sleeper's head. All of them point the same way. She's done the Sundial as well. She'd like you to look at it, and tell her what they're all pointing at.
- The Tessen children are standing on the Quay, all of them, facing up at the Lamp and speaking together in that long careful phrase. They've reached the end of it, for the first time. They stop. A small girl turns around and looks up at you with interest, and she says, in plain speech, "You're late."
- Chief Marrow has found a wrench on a shelf in an empty berth, and it fits his ruined hand exactly. It fits the missing fingers, somehow. He hasn't sworn in two days, and his crew are terrified. He looks up from a panel, perfectly gently, and asks you if you've ever really listened to how quiet she runs here.
- On the Veshti landing of the Stair there's a long table set for twelve, with something on the plates that's gone to dust a very long time ago. The Veshti rating with you has gone white. She runs a finger along the table's edge, finds the place where it's worn and sits down in a chair that fits her as if she'd always had it. "Oh," she says. "We've been here."
- An old man from the fleet gets up from his row on the Long Quay, which takes him a long time, and he comes across to you. He can't feel his wife's name. It's still there, he knows what it is, but it doesn't hurt any more, and he wants you to tell him how to make it hurt again. Nobody else in the row looks up.
- Ensign Pell wants to go Past the Lamp, on the long line, alone. She's the only pilot left who hasn't stopped caring about flying. She's talking too fast, and she'd like you to know that she never finished flight school, and that it doesn't matter now. Someone has to look, and the line's only forty kilometers long.
- You climb all the way up. In the Highest Room a silver figure is standing at attention beside the board, wearing Commander Voss's face. The real one's two steps behind you, breathing hard. It says "Captain", exactly as she does, as though it hasn't finished deciding. Then it asks you, with enormous tiredness, whether you have the watch.
- Father Bastian Roe is reading the list of the dead to an empty hall on the Meridian, the way he does every tenth day. He's wide awake, and he's oddly cheerful. The peace doesn't seem to take on him, he says, as he hasn't got anything for it to get a grip on. It turns out that there's a use for that. He'd like you to come and hear the rest.
- Garrick Fenn is broadcasting to a fleet that's stopped listening. He asks if you'd give him a real bulletin, something bad, something true. If you haven't got one, he says, he's prepared to make one up, as he's done before. He thinks a fire might do it, a small one. He's quite serious, and it might actually work.
- Admiral Okonkwo-Reyes has brought her six ships in, and she's standing on the Quay in full dress uniform, the only person upright for a hundred meters, demanding to see the government. There's no government here. There's only a cache, long since looted, under a name she once signed. People keep on offering her a seat. "Noted for the court-martial," she says, with her hand on her pocket, and she doesn't sit down.

Rumors:
- I don't know why we were all so worried.
- I slept so well. Did you sleep well? Everyone slept so well.
- It's warm. Nobody's stealing anything. My boy's stopped asking about his father. Isn't that nice?
- There's room for everyone. Have you seen the berths? There's room for everyone who ever lived.
- I left the rations out on the deck, and nobody's touched them. I'm not hungry anyway.
- Somebody said that we ought to call a meeting. I think it was yesterday.
- My daughter's drawn the same thing every day since we got here. It's the Lamp, but she's drawn it from behind. How would she have seen it from behind?
- Whoever built this place left in a hurry, or else they didn't leave. There's a mug on a table up on the third landing, with a ring in the bottom of it. It's still a bit warm.
- I went up as far as the fifth landing. All of the doors are different sizes. Who builds a house for everyone? I mean, who would you be expecting?
- Don't go past the Lamp. There's nothing out there, and I mean that: nothing. You can only tell the old ships are there by what they blot out, and there's nothing to blot out.
- You should sit down, Captain. You look so tired. Everyone else has sat down.
- Nobody's on the bridge of the Sundial. The Steward's asleep in the beans, and she's smiling. Has anyone ever seen her smile?
- The Commander's still awake. I don't think that she's slept at all since we got here. Somebody ought to tell her that it's fine.
