In a line:
A lawless asteroid warren of smugglers, raiders and very good noodle stalls.

The feel of it:
Chili broth, hot solder and the mineral damp of rock that has never seen a sun. Tunnels were cut by whoever needed one, so no two levels agree on which way is down, and every junction has a stall, a shrine to somebody's dead captain and a person watching who goes past. Nothing here has a price; it has a debt, and everyone can tell you to the credit what you owe them.

Districts:
The Warrens: Miles of hand-cut tunnel hung with cables and washing. Talia Sen's noodle stall sits at the busiest junction, and it's busy for reasons that have nothing to do with noodles.
Quill's Parlor: A low warm room with embroidered cushions, tea and a great many very quiet people. Mother Quill receives visitors here and insists that they eat something.
Raiders' Row: A long docking gallery where raiders repaint their ships between jobs. Rook Tannis's Second Opinion has the best berth, and nobody has ever asked her to move.
The Tally Wall: A tunnel whose whole length is scratched with names and sums. Any debt carved there is enforceable by anyone who wants to buy it.
The Sump: The bottom level, where the warren's water, waste and unwanted people all end up. Good for hiding, and very bad for coming back from.

Complications:
- Someone has carved your fleet's name into the Tally Wall for a debt you've never heard of, and a collector has already bought it.
- Rook Tannis has decided that today is the day she'd like to have a friendly brawl with the captain of the fleet, in public.
- A pressure door drops in the Warrens, and three rival crews find themselves sealed in with you and short of air.
- Mother Quill has sent you a lovely basket of food. Everyone who sees it knows that it means you're now in her debt.
- A Hegemony infiltrator has been made, and the crowd hunting him decides that you must be with him.
- Your forged papers turn out to have been made by the same forger who's just been sold out to someone else.

Secrets:
- Talia Sen's broth orders are a code: the garnish on a bowl tells a runner what to report and to whom, and half the warren is on her payroll without knowing it.
- Rook Tannis has quietly bought eleven of the debts on the Tally Wall owed by Quill's oldest lieutenants. On the day she moves against Quill, they'll be standing aside.
- The Sump has a sealed Concord customs vault at its lowest point, predating the Syndicate. Quill has never been able to open it, and she'll pay extremely well for anyone who can.

Encounters:
- Talia Sen ladles you a bowl you didn't ask for and tells you it's on the house, which on Kessara is a terrifying sentence. She adds a pinch of something green, and a runner at the far counter gets up and leaves.
- Rook Tannis blocks a tunnel with her shoulders, tells you a terrible joke about a Thessan and a tax collector, and waits. She wants to see whether you'll laugh, hit her or walk around. She has different plans for each.
- A collector in a very clean coat approaches you and politely explains that he's purchased the debt of a dead fleet captain, and that the debt transfers to the fleet. He has it in writing, and he's prepared to accept a shuttle.
- Mother Quill's great-niece, about eleven, is selling you a route map out of a basket and overcharging you with great skill. She drops her voice and adds that she'd like to go to school somewhere there are schools.
- A raider crew in the Row is repainting their ship and arguing about what to call it. Their old name was the name of the fleet ship they gutted last winter, and one of them has just recognized your uniform.
- An old man on the Tally Wall is scraping out his own name with a chisel, and he's almost done. That's a crime here. He looks up and asks you whether you'd be kind enough to stand in front of him for a minute.
- A runaway Hegemony conscript in a stolen coat begs you for passage. He's nervous, sincere and very well informed about your ship's departure time, which he shouldn't be.
- Two noodle-stall owners are fighting a duel with ladles of boiling broth over a recipe, and it's drawing bets. One of them offers you a cut of the side bets if you'll stand next to her rival and ask loud questions about his supplier.
- A deserter from your own fleet, now with a raider tattoo still healing on his neck, buys you a drink and asks how his sister's doing on the Sundial. He's asking for a reason, and it isn't entirely love.
- Quill invites you to tea and asks after your health with real concern. She then mentions, in passing, a shipment of medicine you'll need in about six weeks, a number she shouldn't know.
- A fence in the Warrens is selling a salvaged fleet logbook that names everyone who was left behind at a certain evacuation. Three people are bidding for it, and at least one wants it burned.
- Down in the Sump a little group of refugees has been sold passage to the fleet by a man who's taken their money and vanished. They've been waiting nine days. They don't know you're the fleet's captain until a child points at your insignia.
- A pair of Hegemony agents in very bad smuggler disguises are trying to buy a Resonant. They ask you for directions. The seller, frightened, is waiting at the very place they're asking about.
- A street kid sells you a treasure map to the Beacon, one of dozens going round the warrens, with a good story and a bad forgery on it. For another ten credits, she'll tell you what a certain fence is paying triple for: anything carved in pale stone, in the style of the little pin on Mother Quill's shawl.
- Mother Quill and Talia Sen sit on opposite sides of a noodle counter, beaming at each other. Quill hasn't paid for a bowl in twenty years, and Talia hasn't asked. Quill pats the stool next to her, dear, and suggests that you might like to settle her bill, and both of them are very interested in what you do next.
- A Syndicate bookkeeper with a shaking hand presses a chip into your palm in a crowd. It lists who's bought what on the Tally Wall this year, and it could end a war or start one. Then he walks away, very fast, and two people follow him.

Rumors:
- Eat at Talia's. Pay in cash. Don't ask her what the green stuff is, and if you see her put it in your bowl, leave by a different tunnel.
- Mother Quill's got the only fuel depot big enough for your whole fleet. Everyone knows it's out there. Nobody I've met has seen it and kept their ship.
- The old lady's fences are paying silly money for anything carved in that pale stone, the kind of thing she wears on her shawl. She says that it hums. She's selling Beacon maps by the dozen with one hand, and buying rocks with the other, so which one do you reckon she believes in?
- Tannis is going to take the whole Syndicate one of these days. You didn't hear that here. Actually, you can say you heard it from anyone you like; I've got a list.
- Don't ever let the old lady feed you, dear. I've watched hard men walk in for a chat and walk out owing her a moon.
- A marked debt doesn't die with the person. My mother's has been sold four times, and they're very polite about it, and I was six when she died.
- They say there's a door at the bottom of the Sump that opens onto a Concord vault. They also say the last three crews that went down for it are still down there, and they're not complaining.
- If you want a Hegemony warrant to disappear, you need a clerk at Bastion Nine, and there's a woman there who's got a very soft heart. Or so I hear. For now.
- The ghostweave they're selling in the Warrens is half fake. The real suits don't rustle. If you can hear it in the shop, you'll be hearing it when the guards do.
- The Second Opinion went out last month with forty crew and came back with forty-one. Nobody's saying who the extra one is, and Tannis laughs when you ask.
- A Choir drone got into the lower tunnels last winter. The Syndicate sold tickets to the fight, and the ticket money's what paid for the new pumps.
- Word is that the Hegemony's offering a thousand a head for Ilari, no questions asked. If I were an Ilari, I wouldn't be walking around in a place where a thousand buys a lot of questions not asked.
- A fellow on Raiders' Row swears he saw one of the Concord's own warships out past the Ghostlight, running dark and painted out. He'd had a few. It doesn't mean he's wrong.
- Half the forged papers in known space come out of one back room down on the eighth level. She's blind, you know. Does it all by touch, and she's never made a mistake that she didn't mean to.
