# Where We're Going
kind: main
places: haven, meridian, fleet
people: halm, voss, tharel, naomi, garrick, odessa, pell
factions: fleet
after: main_the_old_scholar

The lead:
Speaker Halm has called the full council to the Meridian's Long Mess, and he's let it be known that the chair at the head of the table is the captain's. He would like the captain to tell thirty thousand people where they're going, in words they can repeat.

What's really going on:
The end of the first act. Halm's question is the fair one: what is the Beacon, and what is the fleet following it toward? The captain knows, by now, that it has two layers and that the top one is in Concord Navy format, and most of the fleet doesn't. Halm wants a mandate or a scapegoat, and either will do. Tharel can be fetched to the table, and he'll say what he can stand to: the Navy layer, and nothing about the lattice under it, and a sharp captain will see him flinch at the question. A child's drawing of the Beacon is on the table: a girl on the Sundial drew it as a lighthouse with a door, and Halm brought it because he knows exactly what it does to a room. The fleet's hope is real, and it's holding everyone together, and that's what will make it so hard to take away later. Garrick Fenn is in the room with his recorder, and whatever's said is going out on his channel tonight. If the fleet has fuel, the council will vote to follow the Beacon, and the first act is over. If it hasn't, the council votes to find fuel first, and the act ends when the fuel's found. If the captain says nothing, or lies badly, Halm abstains, which is how he votes twice. Arc clues: none new. The act moves to 2 when this and main_two_weeks_of_fuel are both settled.

How it might go:
- The Long Mess cleared and set as a chamber, forty-one ships' worth of representatives on benches, and Halm at the foot of the table so that the head is the captain's. A child's drawing on the table, under a cup.
- The question. 'We'd all like to know, Captain, what exactly you're leading us toward.' He never answers a question; he asks this one twice.
- Tharel, if he's sent for, in a borrowed coat, telling the council about the Navy layer in plain words, and not one word about what's under it. Anyone watching him closely sees him flinch when the captain asks.
- The drawing. A lighthouse with a door in it, and a ship going in. The room goes quiet in a way that's hard to argue with.
- The vote: follow the Beacon, find fuel first, or hear the Hegemony out. Fenn's recorder is running, and Voss says nothing until it's over.

Ways through:
- Talker: tell them the truth in words they can repeat (leadership, persuasion). Say 'two layers' out loud and the room splits, and a leader can bring it back together.
- Keeper of secrets: tell them the Navy layer and keep the lattice back (deception, composure). It works tonight. Later it's the thing the fleet asks why they weren't told.
- Scholar: have Tharel do the talking (lore), and watch him. A lore check lets the captain hear what he isn't saying.
- Plain: say you don't know, and that you're going to find out. Halm hates it, Voss respects it, and the room follows the captain who said it.
- Fighter: there's no fight unless the room turns, and it can, if the captain calls the council cowards with Fenn recording.

Checks that fit:
- leadership:hard to carry a room that has just heard the words 'two layers'
- persuasion:moderate to get Halm to call the vote the captain wants
- deception:moderate to keep the lattice out of the room without anyone noticing the gap
- lore:moderate to see what Tharel flinches at
- composure:moderate to keep still while Fenn reads the captain's own words back to them

If it comes to a fight:
- mutineer if the captain's words turn the room into a brawl
