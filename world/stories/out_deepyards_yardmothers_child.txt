# The Yardmother's Child
kind: job
places: deepyards
people: ilkin, marrow, pell
factions: families, fleet
level: 2

The lead:
Yardmother Ilkin Ao-Vash owns the only drydock left that can refit a frigate, and she does not deal with planet-born strangers. She does, however, deal with family. She studies you for a long moment from her webbed chair, with her back to the window, then asks how you'd feel about being adopted.

What's really going on:
She means it, legally. Under Long Family law only blood or adopted kin can book a berth on the Long Drydock. The ceremony is real and it's binding: the captain becomes a child of Ao-Vash, with all the rights and duties, and Ilkin starts calling them "little one" in front of their crew. The duties come out one by one, and each is a real cost. A child of the Family doesn't stand on a planet for a year and a day. They eat with the Family when they're in port, and it's very long and very cold. They take on the Family's feud with Ao-Rel, nine generations old. And they owe their mother obedience, within reason, as she defines reason. Sennet Ao-Vash, Ilkin's sharp and elegant grandchild, greets their new relation with perfect courtesy and a long list of small humiliations. The adoption only opens the door. The refit's real price comes next, offered as if it's a favor, and it half is: two hundred of the fleet's orphans, to be raised as Long Family children, with new names, warm beds and a life in the yards. And the "wait, what?" is at the end of a corridor: a captain who wanders the Hearth Rings finds them lit, warm and very tidy, with ancestors' names on every rail and nobody there. Ring after ring. Places set for meals nobody eats. Births have been falling for a century, more than half the rings are empty, and in three generations there will be no one left to own the yards. Ilkin isn't collecting a fee. She's trying to save her people, and she'd take the captain as well if she could keep them.

How it might go:
- The Rinse: hours of being scrubbed by tall bored attendants, to get the planet off. Then the offer, with dark ships hanging in a blue glow and nobody looking at the captain directly. Dunn Marrow can see the drydock from the viewport, and he's crying a little and pretending not to.
- The ceremony: a long, cold, weightless ritual, where the captain's hair is cut, their old name is recorded in a book, and they're told what they've just agreed to. Ensign Pell Asante tries hard not to laugh, and fails.
- Family dinner. A table with no gravity, thirty relatives, far more place settings than people, and eleven courses that are all forms of algae. Sennet asks how the captain is finding being alive. Every question is a test, and nobody says what's being tested.
- The price. Two hundred children, named as if it's nothing, the morning after the Meridian's hull has been opened and can't fly. Meanwhile a formal insult arrives from Ao-Rel, on very good paper, because they've heard about the new child.
- The empty rings, and what the captain says to Ilkin once they've seen them. She doesn't deny it. She keeps her back to the window and asks how many children starved on the fleet last month.

Ways through:
- Accept and play it straight: lore, composure and persuasion to learn the customs, survive dinner and earn a place in the family for real, then bargain as family over the children.
- Accept and work the angles: deception, commerce and awareness to figure out what's really going on, find the empty rings, and maybe make an ally of Sennet instead of an enemy.
- Refuse, or find a way around it: commerce, void_ops and engineering at the Ice Cut, to fix the ship in pieces, in the dark, with stolen hours from low-branch riggers who like the Meridian's chief engineer more than they like their relatives.

Checks that fit:
- lore:moderate to get through a Long Family ceremony without giving offense
- composure:hard to sit through dinner with Sennet Ao-Vash
- void_ops:moderate to pass the zero-gravity family trials
- commerce:very_hard to change what the refit costs
- awareness:moderate to count the place settings and start wondering about the rings
- deception:moderate to make Sennet believe you're no threat

Where it can end up:
two_hundred_children_come_to_rings: If two hundred children come to the rings, they're warm and fed and loved, with new names, and the Meridian gets a real refit: more hull, better engines and perhaps a second gun. Some of them write. Some of the letters are signed with the new names. The Children's Offer at Vantor may be pulling from the same list.
captain_talks_her_down: If the captain talks her down, say to volunteers, or to families who come together, or to a mixed settlement of the fleet in the empty rings, then the Long Families change for good. It's a hopeful outcome, and most of the Family will hate it.
captain_breaks_ground_rule_by_landing: If the captain breaks the ground rule by landing on Tessen or anywhere else, Ilkin hears about it within a day. Being disowned is a formal process, with a very cold letter, and the refit stops wherever it is.
captain_refuses_everything: If the captain refuses everything, the fleet goes on with its battered flagship, and Ilkin's offer stays open, with a slightly raised eyebrow, for when things get worse. Sennet, won over or not, stays a very courteous problem.

If nobody steps in:
after 30 days: There's a queue now for Yardmother Ilkin's drydock at the Deepyards, the only one left that can refit a frigate, and everyone in it is family.
