What's really going on:
The recorder holds eleven minutes that Voss can't remember. What she remembers is waking to an empty ship, and silver figures at parade rest making a lane to the boat bay. One with Lt. Ibarra's face saluted, and she didn't salute back. The audio from her cabin shows what came first. The Choir came to her gently, held her half asleep for eleven minutes, and many soft voices asked, "What must be kept?" Her own flat voice answered, "The line. Hold the line until you're relieved." Then it set her down. Why did they let her go? The recorder's answer is chilling: because they already had her. She was copied, and her body simply didn't die. So her scans were always clean, and she really is only herself. There's just another Ilya Voss in the song, with every code and picket plan she knew up to that day. That's only half the story: the other half is the Tarn's deck log (main_on_station), which says why the Choir was so careful. Sable sells a Concord Intelligence file on the Tarn's last telemetry burst, and that file has a word for people like her: walkers. Eleven were on file. What Sable won't sell at any price is why the Tarn was where she was (sable_canary).

How it might go:
- A Guild crew found the recorder pod drifting at the edge of the Long Silence, and nobody has found the ship it came from. The bidders gather at the Graveyard. Factor Elsbeth Crane's rules are simple and have never been broken: sealed bids, payment on the nail, and the recorder stays in a strongroom hulk until then. She also holds a dummy claim on the Tarn through a third party, and part of the point of this auction is to see who turns up.
- Who else wants it: Inspector Moor, politely. A man buying for Speaker Halm. Voss herself, on leave, in civilian clothes that fit her like a uniform, with everything she owns in one envelope.
- Someone moves early. Claim-jumpers hired through a cut-out go for the strongroom before the bids open, and Crane regards this as everybody's problem.
- The captain hears the recording, maybe with Voss in the room. The damaged audio takes work. The last ninety seconds are very clear.
- The practical horror arrives afterward: every code and patrol plan she knew at the Fall has to be changed, and that can't be done without saying why.

Ways through:
- Win it honestly: commerce at the sealed auction, paid in fleet stores or favors. Lore helps more than money: old Guild salvage law gives a ship's last surviving officer first refusal on its log, if anyone can find the clause.
- Steal it: void_ops and stealth into the strongroom hulk, with the diver Rusk as a guide if he can be hired, and a problem if he can't. Breaking a Guild seal has consequences that last for years.
- Go around it: Sable, at Haven, sells the walker file. They answer with a price, and this price is an unnamed favor to be collected later. If they're asked why the Tarn was where she was, the glass stops moving, and there is no price for that.
- Back Voss: she means to bid everything she has, then go in alone if she loses. Leadership or persuasion keeps her from doing something that can't be undone, or the captain becomes her accomplice.

Where it can end up:
voss_hears: If Voss hears it, she listens once, asks for the last ninety seconds again, then goes back to the Meridian and rewrites every code she has ever known, overnight. She stops the scans. She sleeps no better.
captain_buries: If the captain buries it, Voss goes on scanning and the old codes stay live. One day the Choir uses a Concord recognition code it shouldn't have, and the captain will know exactly how.
inspector_moor_gets: If Inspector Moor gets it, it goes through channels, and the Regency, which very much wants the one person the Choir ever let walk, starts asking after Commander Voss by name. If Halm's man gets it, it comes up in council at the worst possible time, phrased very kindly.
reaches_garrick_fenn: If it reaches Garrick Fenn, the whole fleet knows within a day. Tarrow asks for custody. Some of the crew salute Voss harder than before, and some won't share a lift with her.
