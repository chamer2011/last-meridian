# The Downstairs Crew
kind: crew
places: meridian
people: naomi, marrow, voss, aduro, tarrow, odessa
factions: fleet

The lead:
Naomi Cass is standing in your doorway to inform you that Section Fourteen will not be vented tomorrow. She's twelve, and she says it the way Commander Voss says things.

For anyone who isn't the captain:
Naomi Cass has told everyone who'll listen that Section Fourteen will not be vented tomorrow. She's twelve, and she says it the way Commander Voss says things.

What's really going on:
Naomi isn't the only stowaway. Thirty-one children live in the Meridian's crawlspaces, all from the creche ship Ember Day, pushed through the Meridian's hatch during the evacuation by a nursery synthetic who then went back for more. (That's Orchard, now in Marrow's aft hold. They visit her every night. When she counts to thirty-one at lights-out and says "all present", she's right: see crew_marrow_aft_hold.) They have laws painted on a duct, an elected council, a court, and a doctor who is ten. Their chief engineer, who is nine, is why Marrow's ducts work better than they should. He's been telling people the ship is being kind to him. Naomi is their captain, and the oldest by two years. Section Fourteen is their dormitory, and Marrow's hull work will vent it tomorrow. They won't come out, because the last time adults found one of them, she was sent to the school ship Wintermere and stopped talking. Quartermaster Brandt has been feeding them on paper for months, as thirty-one crates, miscellaneous, perishable, and she has never gone to look. Their little constitution is better than Halm's, which is a problem all by itself.

How it might go:
- Naomi's order at the doorway. She won't explain. She'll negotiate, but only with the captain and only in person, which means crawling.
- The tour: a long crawl through the warm dark. Painted rules. A nine-year-old who corrects the captain's grip on a wrench. A court in session over a stolen sock. They call the captain "the Upstairs Captain".
- The thing they aren't saying: a boy with a fever, three days along, and a ten-year-old doctor who's out of ideas and too proud to say so.
- Treaty talks. They've drafted one, and it has clauses. Voss, if told, reads it, says nothing and corrects a typo. Clause nine is in different handwriting: the Upstairs Captain will find out if a man called Cass is on any manifest. He isn't, on any ship in the fleet, and Quartermaster Brandt has known that for months.
- Tomorrow comes, and this is a warship with children in its walls. What happens when the Choir does?

Ways through:
- Diplomacy: persuasion with their council. They vote on everything, at length, and can't be bribed with anything but the truth and sweets.
- Go in and see: athletics and stealth through the crawlspaces, medicine for the sick boy, and a fight with what's been nesting in the deep ducts.
- Engineering: reroute the hull work around Section Fourteen. Marrow is outraged and then delighted. "My ghost is NINE?"
- Make it official: leadership to swear them in as ship's cadets, with rations, lessons and a drill for when the alarm goes. Voss says nothing and has thirty-one cut-down uniforms made.
- Send them off by order: intimidation. Some go. The rest go deeper, and the captain has just made an enemy of people who know every secret on the ship.

Where it can end up:

Halm demands they be moved, Garrick Fenn's story on it is the most popular he's ever aired, and if the ship takes a hit the captain will have to live with what they chose.
