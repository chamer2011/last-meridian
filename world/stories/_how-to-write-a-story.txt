HOW TO WRITE A STORY
====================

Every story the game can offer is one file in this folder, named by the story's id: two_weeks_of_fuel.txt is the
story main_two_weeks_of_fuel. These are the game master's notes, not the words the player reads: the AI runs the
story from them, and the written scene in world/scenes, if there is one, plays it out. The Story board in the game
(The story, then Story board) shows and changes these same files as boxes and arrows, and npm run check-story reads
them all and says what's wrong, with the file and the line.

A file starts with the title, then a few lines about the story, then its parts, each under a heading that ends with
a colon. Here is a short one, with every kind of line:

    # Two Weeks of Fuel
    kind: main                          (main, crew, dilemma, mystery, exploration, survival, politics, job, faction)
    places: kessara, haven, cinder      (where it happens: the first is where the lead comes up)
    people: quill, tannis, marrow       (ids from world/characters)
    factions: drift, fleet, hegemony    (ids from world/factions.json)
    level: 3                            (leave it out for 1: the level the player should have)
    after: main_the_old_scholar         (a story that has to be over first; leave it out if none)
    waits: Needs the fleet at the Harbor.     (why its clock never starts on its own; rare)
    clock from: 30 days                 (when its clock starts, if not by the level; rare)

    The lead:
    Chief Marrow has done the sums three times: the fleet has two weeks of fuel.

    For anyone who isn't the captain:
    The fleet's down to two weeks of fuel, and everyone on the docks knows it.

    What's really going on:
    Quill owns the only depot within reach, and she knows it.

    How it might go:
    - Tea in Quill's Parlor, on embroidered cushions.
    - Marrow's numbers get worse.

    Ways through:
    - Talker: haggle with Quill (commerce, persuasion), or play her against Corvane.
    - Fighter: take the depot with Tannis's help (tactics, gunnery, melee).

    Checks that fit:
    - commerce:hard to put a limit on Quill's markers in writing

    If it comes to a fight:
    - drift_raider and syndicate_collector if the depot raid goes wrong

    Where it can end up:
    sign_with_quill: If they sign with Quill, the fleet has fuel and Quill has forty-one markers.
    raid_depot: If they raid the depot, the fleet is fueled and at war with the Syndicate.

    Facts it can leave:
    kessara_closed, after raid_depot: Kessara Drift is closed to the fleet since the depot.
    markers_limited: Mother Quill's markers have a limit, in writing.

    What on the record changes it:
    ? ended main_the_old_scholar quills_people_get_him -> Tharel is in Quill's hands, so the price goes up.

    If nobody steps in:
    after 18 days: Mother Quill's depot has started selling to whoever asks first.
      lead: The fuel's still there, but the depot is selling it to anyone who asks.
      at kessara: fuel -15
    after 45 days: The council took a shipment of fuel from Legate Corvane without the captain.
      if: not ended main_two_weeks_of_fuel
      fleet: fuel +3, morale -1
      big
      fact hegemony_transponders: Every ship in the fleet carries a Hegemony transponder.
      ending: take_corvanes_fuel

    Consequences:
    ? ended main_two_weeks_of_fuel raid_depot, after 7 days: Kessara Drift's docks are closed to the fleet.
      place: kessara
      fact kessara_closed: Kessara Drift is closed to the fleet since the depot.

    Rewards:
    Fuel for the fleet, and a place on Quill's list.

The parts, in the order the game master reads them
--------------------------------------------------

The lead is what the player hears when the story comes up. For anyone who isn't the captain is the same for a
player who isn't in charge; leave it out and they get the lead. What's really going on is for the game master only.
How it might go is the beats, Ways through the routes the player might take, Checks that fit the skill checks
(a skill, a colon, easy, moderate or hard, then what it's for), and If it comes to a fight who they'd fight (enemy
ids from world/enemies). Each of those is a list: one line per item, starting with - and a space. Rewards is one
line of what the player gets.

A part that has nothing in it can be left out, except The lead, What's really going on and Rewards.

Where it can end up is the story's endings, each with a name the rest of the game uses: lowercase letters, digits
and underscores. The AI ends the story with one of these names, the written scene does with * ending, the record
keeps it, and other stories ask for it with ended main_two_weeks_of_fuel raid_depot.

Facts it can leave names the facts this story can put on the record, each with a line of plain words that the
player reads in the Quests menu. The AI uses these names, and a scene that sets the flag without words gets the
line from here. Add ", after an_ending" when one ending in particular leaves it; the board draws it from there.

What on the record changes it is how this story reacts to what happened before: a condition, written the same way
as in a scene (flag a_fact, ended a_story, ended a_story its_ending, not ..., and ...), then -> and what changes in
this story. The game master only sees the ones that hold when the story begins.

If nobody steps in is the story's clock: what happens by itself, on the day given, if nobody has taken the story up.
Every line under a stage is optional: lead is what the lead says from then on, if is a condition the stage needs
(skipped otherwise, so two stages on the same day with opposite ifs are a fork), place is where it happens if not
the story's first place, big means everyone who knows the place hears it, at a_place changes what a place has
(food, fuel, medicine, parts) or who holds it (a faction, by its id), fleet changes the fleet (morale, food,
medicine, parts, fuel, pursuit, ships, picked), named marks one ship lost or gone, fact puts a fact on the record,
and ending ends the story by one of its own endings.

Consequences follow from the record, whether or not anyone has taken the story up: once the condition holds, that
many days later the news happens, once, and if the condition stops holding before then the count starts over.
They take the same lines as a stage, except if and ending.

Where to look when something's wrong
------------------------------------

Run npm run check-story. It names the file and the line for anything it can't read, and it checks what the parts
say against the rest of the world: a story, an ending, a place or a fact that doesn't exist, and a fact that
nothing leaves. The game still starts with a story file that has a mistake in it, as far as the file can be read.
