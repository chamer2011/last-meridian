# The Chart Nobody Believes
kind: exploration
places: deepyards
people: tamsin, ilkin, pell, grann
factions: families, fleet
level: 4

The lead:
Exiled navigator Tamsin Ao-Rel has a chart of a route the Long Families swear doesn't exist, and a ship too small to fly it. They'll show you where it goes if you take them along. "Promise not to laugh when we get there," they say. "Or cry. People have tried both."

For anyone who isn't the captain:
Exiled navigator Tamsin Ao-Rel has a chart of a route the Long Families swear doesn't exist, and a ship too small to fly it. They'll show anyone where it goes who can take them along. "Promise not to laugh when we get there," they say. "Or cry. People have tried both."

What's really going on:
The route is one of the backward lines on the Ao-Vash route plate that Tamsin copied, though they've been selling it as their own work. It threads a fold in the dark that doesn't show on any survey, the ship's clocks come back wrong, and it comes out at a mild green world that Tamsin calls Old Sill. There are stone terraces with very tall doorways, and thousands of hectares of orchard gone wild, heavy with fruit. Nine generations ago the Veshti lived on planets like everyone else, and this was one of their farm worlds. The Families say a blight came, that they fled to their ships and swore on their dead never to touch soil again, and over the years they turned shame into doctrine. (That isn't the whole of it: deep_the_thin_road has what really happened on their greatest world, and Tamsin won't speak of that here.) Here's the part Tamsin doesn't know, because they never landed: not everyone left. There are about forty people living on the terraces, short and thick-boned by Veshti standards, shaped by generations of gravity: the descendants of the ones who were too sick or too stubborn to board. They've tended the orchards all this time, and they've kept a long table laid in the old hall, with a place set for each family's name. They have been expecting the ships to come back. It's been nine generations, and they'd like to know what kept them.

How it might go:
- The jump itself. A route that frightens Ensign Pell Asante, a gap that isn't on any sensor, and Tamsin in the right-hand seat, calmly eating something and calling out numbers from memory.
- Coming out. A green world on the viewscreen. Tamsin goes completely quiet, and then starts to laugh, and it's a bad laugh. Is the blight still here? There's real science to do before anyone takes off a helmet.
- The terraces and the fruit. Doorways built for very tall people, worn by generations of hands, and Steward Grann Tull on the comm demanding soil samples in a voice that shakes.
- Someone comes out of the old hall with a basket: short, wide, very old and not at all surprised. "You'll be from the ships," she says, in a Veshti so ancient that only Tamsin can follow it. "You're late. Sit down." The table is laid.
- What to do with this. A food source that can't be shared without spreading its secret, a people who believe they've been waiting for kin, and a whole culture out in the ice who'll hate the captain for ever finding it.

Ways through:
- Fly it: navigation and piloting to run the fold, with Tamsin or without them, and survival to get people down safely and find out what's safe to eat.
- Read it: lore and science to follow the old records, test for the blight and piece together the Families' true history.
- Open the door: persuasion and commerce with the terrace people and Ilkin Ao-Vash, to decide who gets told, who gets fed, and who gets to come home.

Checks that fit:
- navigation:very_hard to run the route without Tamsin's help
- piloting:hard to thread the fold in a frigate
- science:hard to determine whether the old blight is still dangerous
- lore:moderate to read the terraces and understand what they are
- survival:moderate to live off an untended orchard on a strange world
- persuasion:very_hard to bring this news to a Yardmother

If it comes to a fight:
- void_stalker lurking in the fold if the crossing goes wrong, which running cold or an abrupt burn can usually lose. Nothing on the terraces wants to fight.

Where it can end up:
secret_kept_shared_with_few: If the secret is kept and shared with a few, Old Sill feeds part of the fleet, a little more each season, and Grann Tull gets her first real orchard. Secrets like that get harder to keep as more mouths eat from it.
long_families_learn_truth: If the Long Families learn the truth, their faith cracks. Some Veshti come to see the table for themselves, weeping. Others swear to burn it. Ilkin wants to speak to the captain alone, and she has never been so polite.
blight_still_captain_brings_back: If the blight is still there and the captain brings it back, the Sundial has a very bad season. There's a cure in the terrace people's long practice, if somebody asks.
captain_leaves_undisturbed: If the captain leaves them undisturbed, Tamsin stays behind at the table, in a seat with their family's name on it, and sends the chart back with the ship.

If nobody steps in:
after 38 days: Long Family riggers at the Deepyards have started asking after a chart, and after whoever's been selling copies of it.
  lead: The Long Families have started asking after Tamsin Ao-Rel's chart, and they're not asking nicely. Tamsin says the offer stands, and that you might want to decide while they've still got a ship, however small.

Rewards:
A nav_slate with the fold on it, a real and hopeful new source of fleet food and morale, seed and rootstock for the Sundial, and Tamsin Ao-Rel's lasting friendship. It's also proof to a very angry people that the planet-born were right.
