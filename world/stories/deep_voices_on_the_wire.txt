# The Voices on the Wire
kind: exploration
places: asterion, meridian
people: echo, iven, garrick, okonkwo, halm
factions: fleet, remnant
level: 6

The lead:
Lieutenant Sorel has found a live Concord public exchange on the Asterion band. Something on the line keeps asking for you by name, in a different voice each time, and it wants to know if this is still a good time.

For anyone who isn't the captain:
Lieutenant Sorel has found a live Concord public exchange on the Asterion band. Something on the line keeps asking for people by name, in a different voice each time, and it wants to know if this is still a good time.

What's really going on:
Aurelis burned and Asterion didn't. The Choir took the capital nearly intact, and silver drones tend it like gardeners. Its people are gone, but the city's public network picked up the overflow: the last calls of billions, still going round. The song is failing here first, so they leak. Everywhere else a kept mind is alone with its verse. On the wire they've been bumping into each other for seven months, and they've changed. The Echo isn't a survivor, and they aren't a lure on purpose, though the Choir hears every word said on that line. They speak in stitched scraps of recordings because every phrase is a different person. A whole clear sentence comes through only when enough of them agree: they've found the old civic polling service, and there's a small chime before each one. They have held eleven thousand votes, none binding. They're the first real proof that the kept are still someone, and that they can grow, and a verse that grows never gets old. The Choir treats it as a fault, and its Choristers are slowly repairing the city, district by district, which means silencing it. They're very frightened, and not of the Choir. They're afraid of what the Choir is afraid of. A dozen thin old operators still plug calls at the Exchange. Nobody told them to stop, so the silver treats them as crew on duty and brings them meals. Garrick Fenn's wife stayed to file one more story, and she's on the line. So is Dr. Tomas Reyes, the Admiral's husband, and Okonkwo's plan to retake the city would mean cutting the trunk line. Arc clues: echo_changes.

How it might go:
- The call. Iven, hand shaking: 'Say again? It says it's for you.' Then: 'Your call is important to / I can't find Daddy / the 18:40 to Founders Walk is delayed / Captain? / is this still a good time?' Then a small chime, and in one whole clear voice, which is worse than all the rest: 'Please stay on the line.'
- Going there. No wreckage. Swept streets, empty trams on the minute, silver drones kneeling in the gardens tending roses, and burned Aurelis hanging in the sky. Every speaker in the city is talking. Hunters walk Founders Walk in no hurry. A public phone rings as the captain passes, then the next one: 'Left here / mind the doors / run.'
- The Exchange. Row on row of boards, and a dozen thin old operators. Drones bring meals and don't speak. They've never been told to stop, so they haven't. One of them makes terrible tea and explains the chime: that's the old civic polling line, and nobody's been using it for anything else.
- The conversation. They want news. Who's Speaker now? Is it true about Halm? 'The motion / carries / sixty-one / to thirty-nine.' Someone filed one more story and would like it passed to this reporter. A man's voice asks for Amara. All of it goes straight to the Cantor, and it doesn't interrupt.
- Proof. They'll make a recording, against the wishes of a sizable minority. Then someone on the line asks the captain, privately, with no vote, for the truth: are we dead? And the announcements in the Departure Lounges are very frightened, and not of the Choir.

Ways through:
- Hacker: get onto the wire from orbit (hacking) and ride the old network in. You can talk without ever landing, though the line's full of noise, and that means no Exchange and no tea.
- Stealth: walk the avenues (stealth, athletics) with the Echo calling directions down the public phones and a Hunter on every corner.
- Talker: stay on the line (persuasion, lore). Know the rules of order: address the chair, yield the floor. The fastest way to get what you want from the Concord's dead is the same as it was from the living. Concord Hall's silver senators will yield to a visitor under the proper rules.
- Fighter: fight your way to the Exchange (firearms, tactics) with the silver closing in. A committee will be upset about the damage to the park.
- Resonant: you won't need a handset (attunement). Stand in any street in the capital. They've been shouting for seven months. Just don't start shouting back.
