The lead:
Lieutenant Tarrow has asked to see you with the door locked. She's holding a folder, and for the first time since you've known her she doesn't seem to want to open it.

What's really going on:
All the threads of the Navy voice converge. The overlay's call sign belongs to CFS Tarn, lost in the first hour of the Fall, and the Tarn's commanding officer was Commander Ilya Voss. She has told no one. Everyone's first conclusion: the Choir has the ship, the Beacon is bait and the XO is theirs. The evidence is excellent. She has lied for six months. She runs secret medical scans at night and wipes the logs. She ordered Lira Vey in writing not to log a contact whose transponder reads CFS Tarn. Iven Sorel hears a dead woman reading the Tarn's crew list with one name left out. A Mimic or the Cantor uses her exact words. Who else ever walked out of a boarded ship? All of it is wrong. She doesn't know why she lived. She woke to an empty ship, with her door open and silver figures at parade rest in the corridor. They made a lane to the boat bay, and one of them saluted with a face she knew. She didn't salute back. She'll tell that plainly if asked, and she'll accept arrest without protest, because that's the regulation and she half believes it herself. Her scans are clean: she's entirely human. The Cantor picks this moment to speak on the Beacon band for the first time, to the whole bridge: 'Captain. Tell the Commander it's all right. We have the deck.' It means that kindly. It's the worst thing it could have said. Sable knows more and isn't saying. Arc clues: hull_number, voss_night_scans, iven_knows_the_fist, cantor_says_captain, spared_map.

How it might go:
- The folder. The call sign, the registry page, Voss's name. Tarrow wants six hours and has a team ready. She has never asked anyone's permission so politely.
- Voss. The captain can confront her alone, on the bridge or in her cabin. She takes off her rank tabs before she sits down, and puts them on the table. Then the plain account, in about nine sentences. 'That's all of it. I don't know why. I've looked.'
- It keeps coming: Iven's notebook, Lira's written order, Naomi's crawlspace testimony, Aduro's clean results. Each one looks worse than the last.
- The Cantor hails the bridge, on an open band. Everyone hears it. It says 'Captain' exactly the way she does.
- It gets out, through Garrick Fenn or the mess decks. A crowd gathers at the brig or the bridge hatch. Okonkwo demands that Voss come to her flag. Halm calls a vote to stop following the Beacon and accept Corvane's terms. The captain has to say something to thirty thousand people, and decide what happens to their XO.

Ways through:
- Leadership: hold the line in public (leadership, composure). She stays in uniform or goes to the brig on the captain's word until there's proof. Tarrow's team has to be faced down (intimidation, tactics).
- Medical and technical: prove what can be proved (medicine, science): she's human, and her scans are clean, and Aduro will swear to it. Recover the logs (hacking). It doesn't prove she isn't theirs. It buys time.
- Resonant: touch her mind, with her permission (attunement). There's no song in her at all, only a closed door and a lot of fear. But a Resonant's word is worth little on this ship, and Tarrow will say so.
- Detective: work the holes. What would a spy actually do? Why would the Choir burn its one agent by saying that on an open band? (lore, tactics, awareness) Then who moved the Tarn? A trip to Sable (commerce, persuasion) or to Prime Six.
- Friend: take Iven to the medbay, get Naomi a real bunk, and listen to what people have been carrying alone (persuasion, medicine). The whole crew has been keeping pieces of this from each other for months.
