The lead:
A nervous Ministry accountant named Pilar Oyelowo passes you a note in a Vantor tea house. She wishes to defect, and it's perfectly legal: there's a form. Nobody has ever been approved, but while Form Nine-C is being processed, no officer of the Ministry is allowed to touch her. It takes seventy-two hours, and they've already started being polite.

For anyone who isn't the captain:
A nervous Ministry accountant named Pilar Oyelowo has been passing notes in a Vantor tea house. She wishes to defect, and it's perfectly legal: there's a form. Nobody has ever been approved, but while Form Nine-C is being processed, no officer of the Ministry is allowed to touch her.

What's really going on:
The law is real. The Hegemony is a government of rules, and its secret police follow them with terrifying exactness. Pilar must collect nine stamps from nine offices across Vantor within seventy-two hours, and in that time Ministry agents will follow her around at a respectful distance, hold doors open and offer her umbrellas. Everybody knows that no form has ever gone through: one office or another always happens to be closed. Pilar has done the accounts for the Ministry's detention service for eleven years, and she stamps the files of the prisoners closed as resettled. She noticed that the number of people moved never matched the rations issued on the far end, and that the ships had no names. She started keeping the names. She has brought the ledgers. The uncomfortable part is that she signed those arrest warrants herself, thousands of them, and at least two families in the fleet would know her name on sight. The twist is Inspector Jaska Moor, who turns up politely at every office with one more question. She's been told to see this through to its proper end. She's also following twelve tons of fuel that don't add up, and she has a brother who was arrested and whose ration line stopped. She'll never say so, and she's slowing down just a little.

How it might go:
- The tea house. Pilar gets out the form, already filled in, in her very neat hand. Two grey-coated gentlemen at the next table raise their cups in greeting.
- Stamp by stamp: a closed office, a queue that has been moved, a clerk who needs a form that requires a stamp from an office that needs this one. The gentlemen help carry her bags.
- Inspector Jaska Moor, with just one more question for the captain, and it's not the one they'd expect. Legate Corvane drops by to say how unfortunate it would be if anyone got the wrong impression.
- Pilar opens her ledgers and shows the captain the columns. She doesn't know where the unmarked ships go. She only knows that the food stops. Then one of the fleet's refugees at Vantor recognizes her.
- Hour seventy-one. The ninth office. Either a stamp, or a very courteous arrest, or something the Ministry didn't plan for.

Ways through:
- Beat the paperwork: lore and persuasion to find the rule that makes the closed offices open, and commerce to buy a clerk's lunch at just the right moment.
- Go around it: stealth and deception to give the grey coats a double, forge a stamp and get Pilar aboard before anyone can politely stop her.
- Make it public: leadership and composure to walk her through Vantor's main square with a broadcast running, and let the Ministry's manners be its own trap, with Garrick Fenn's help if he's around.

If it comes to a fight:
- hegemony_trooper or ministry_blade if the seventy-two hours run out and the captain still has Pilar. Up to that moment, nobody will lay a hand on anyone, and that's the whole point.

Where it can end up:
shes_approved: If she's approved, it's the first time in the Hegemony's history. Pilar goes free, the clerk who stamped the last box is quietly reassigned, and the captain now has the ledgers that lead to Unmarked.
shes_brought_out_illegally: If she's brought out illegally, the fleet gets the ledgers and the Hegemony gets a legal complaint and a real grievance, with hegemony standing dropping and Vael taking a personal interest.
fleet_familys_accusation_heard: If the fleet family's accusation is heard, the captain has to decide if a woman who signed thousands of warrants gets protection, a trial or a push out of the door, while her ledgers are the best evidence anyone has.
fails: If she fails, she goes with a grateful nod to the grey coats and a look back at the captain. The ledgers may still reach the captain later, by a slow and very careful route through Inspector Moor.

If nobody steps in:
