# The Cold Crossing
kind: survival
places: ghostlight
people: marrow, pell, lira, tarrow
factions: fleet
level: 2

The lead:
Things hunt by heat in the Ghostlight, so the whole fleet will cross it cold: reactors down to a whisper, no galleys, no heaters, four days in the dark. On the second night a mother on a grain hauler lights a stove for a sick baby. Ten minutes later Ensign Vey's board shows something long and pale, turning your way.

For anyone who isn't the captain:
Things hunt by heat in the Ghostlight, so ships cross it cold: reactors down to a whisper, no galleys, no heaters, four days in the dark. There's a mother on a grain hauler with a sick baby, who wants to know what's to be done about that.

What's really going on:
Void stalkers come in from the Burn toward any heat at all, and forty-one ships can't stay cold for ever. People light stoves. A family lights one because their baby's chest has gone bad, a captain lights one because his old engineer is dying, and each is a beacon. The moral question is immediate and ugly: does the captain order the family's stove out, send someone to do it, or cut that ship loose from the convoy? Then comes the strange part. On the third day something enormous and very old attaches itself to the Meridian's hull. It's a stalker, far bigger than any on record, pale as a candle, and it wraps itself around the warmth of the main coil and does nothing. It doesn't bite. It just presses close, the way a dog does on a cold night. Dunn Marrow is outraged, Lt. Tarrow wants to burn it off, and the crew have already named it Aunt. Then Ensign Lira Vey notices something on her board: since it attached itself, the other stalkers have stayed well away. They're afraid of it. It's taken the Meridian as its nest, and for as long as it stays, the whole fleet is safe. But a stalker that size has to go back to the cloud, or die, and it's eating the Meridian's heat. Every hour it stays, someone on the Meridian gets colder.

How it might go:
- The order. Forty-one ships going dark, one after another. Breath showing in the corridors. Ensign Pell Asante flying by dead reckoning, with lamps out, and a hand-rung bell from the Cold Market carrying through the hull over a borrowed channel.
- The stove. A family, a sick baby and a stalker turning toward them. The captain has minutes, and the whole fleet is listening on the channel.
- Aunt arrives, and there's an incredible thump on the outer hull. Someone has to go out and look. It's bigger than a shuttle, and it turns its eyeless head toward the suit's heater and then, quite slowly, away.
- The crew takes sides. Aunt is the first good thing that's happened in weeks. A child draws her. Marrow's readings show the main coil starting to suffer. Tarrow has a charge ready and isn't asking twice.
- The edge of the cloud. Aunt won't let go by herself. What does it take, and what does the Meridian look like afterward? There's an old, very faint sound through the hull as she goes, if she goes.

Ways through:
- Hold the line: leadership and composure to keep thirty thousand freezing people dark, and medicine to keep the babies alive some other way, with body heat and shared blankets.
- Find the way: navigation and survival to plot the coldest, shortest line through the Lamp Lanes, and engineering to bleed the fleet's waste heat somewhere that fools a hunter.
- Deal with Aunt: void_ops and science to go out on the hull and understand her, and find a way to keep her or send her back, or firearms and gunnery if that's what the captain decides on, knowing it brings the rest of them.

Checks that fit:
- leadership:hard to order a mother to put out her stove, and be obeyed by everyone listening
- navigation:hard to plot a safe line by dead reckoning
- manifestation:hard to push something that size off a hull without hurting it, and to feel what it feels
- void_ops:moderate to work a hull in the dark beside something that size
- science:moderate to work out why the others stay away from her
- medicine:moderate to keep a sick infant alive without a heater

If it comes to a fight:
- void_stalker if a ship is left burning warm and someone goes across to help. Lamps out and heaters off makes them lose interest within a few minutes, which is the whole idea.

Where it can end up:
fleet_crosses_with_aunt: If the fleet crosses with Aunt, nearly everyone lives, the Meridian's coil is damaged and the crew has a legend. If she's coaxed back into the cloud, the fleet gets a strange ally in the Ghostlight: stalkers leave Meridian's ships alone on later crossings.
shes_burned_off_hull: If she's burned off the hull, the crew never quite forgives the order, and the rest of the stalkers come in at once, for a very long night.
captain_cuts_ship_loose_to_save: If the captain cuts a ship loose to save the others, the stalkers go to that ship and the rest cross safely. That's a ship knowingly left behind, and if the Long Bet is running, a card arrives.
stove_family_saved_cost: If the stove family is saved at a cost, people will talk about it for as long as there's a fleet. The sick baby may live to hear the story, and her family would follow the captain into the Burn itself.

If nobody steps in:
after 30 days: A refugee hauler tried the cold crossing of the Ghostlight by herself. Someone lit a stove on the second night, and that was the last anyone heard.

Rewards:
Safe passage through the Ghostlight later on, fleet morale if it goes well, a bit of pale shed skin that's very useful to Mercy Seven's science team, and some calm_tabs and nutrient_gel saved up by very cold quartermasters.
