# Ward Six Has Stopped Dying
kind: mystery
places: anselm
people: wen, pavel, oskar, tarrow, seven, aduro, tessaly
factions: fleet, quiet
level: 5

The lead:
Nobody has died in Ward Six of Anselm's Mercy in twenty-three days. Dr. Adeyemi should be thrilled. They've asked you to come in person, and to please leave Lieutenant Tarrow behind.

For anyone who isn't the captain:
Nobody has died in Ward Six of Anselm's Mercy in twenty-three days. Dr. Adeyemi should be thrilled. They've started keeping people out of there, and they've asked that nobody tell Lieutenant Tarrow.

What's really going on:
Pavel Ostrov, a piano tuner from Aurelis, woke from a coma humming and doesn't know he does it. The tune, three falling notes and one rising, is a phrase of the song's oldest verse. (If main_visiting_hours has been played, this comes some time after it.) Wen keeps him among the dying in Ward Six on purpose, and here's why: the ward has started humming along without waking, and their vital signs have leveled out. It averaged a death a day, and it has now had none for twenty-three days. The round steadies a failing mind, because holding minds together is exactly what the song is for. The cost: everyone who recovers keeps humming, and they all stay in perfect time, even across ships, with no signal any instrument can find. Two discharged patients on the Tollan Arms keep time with Ward Six to the beat. Attunement can feel a thin thread running from each of them out into the dark. Wen has two hundred more dying patients and wants to move them in next to Pavel. The catch, which nobody will guess: one Choir scout has shifted course toward the fleet, and it isn't hunting. The old frontier stories about silver visitors at deathbeds are true, and it can't bear watching people die where it can't reach them. If crew_pell_letters has been played, bed forty is in this ward.

How it might go:
- The tour: clean, quiet, tired nurses starting to cry for joy. Underneath it, a tune the captain has heard somewhere. Pavel asks when he can see his daughter Mila, and he's lovely. She's seven and alive on the Sundial, and nobody has told her he's awake, because nobody knows what he is.
- Wen's chart. They want the captain's signature to move two hundred patients in. They know exactly what it sounds like.
- Someone matches the tune: Lira, Iven or Aduro. A nurse's child has started humming it at school.
- It gets out. Oskar Lind arrives, asking only to sit with them. Tarrow arrives, asking only for Pavel. Tessaly Marsh turns up on the Gallery with a very large offer for him. Garrick Fenn is a day behind them all.
- The scout's new course comes in. If nobody stops it, there's the night it arrives: a single silver figure walks onto Ward Six, past the nurses, sits down by the worst bed and takes the patient's hand. It doesn't do anything else. Now the captain has to choose.

Ways through:
- Science and medicine: work with Aduro and Seven to isolate the useful phrase and remove whatever carries it, if that's possible. It may take more days than the ward has.
- Attunement: sit with them and find out what the thread is connected to. Manifestation might cut it, one patient at a time, and nobody knows if a patient survives that.
- Security: engineering and tactics to shield the ward, move Anselm's Mercy to the fleet's edge, or quietly station the Meridian between the hospital and the scout.
- People: persuasion to keep out Oskar's followers and Tarrow's marines, and to sit down with Pavel and tell him what he is.
- Face the visitor: composure and persuasion, if it comes. It will answer a polite question gently, and it won't understand why everyone is shouting.

Where it can end up:

stopped: Stopped: Pavel is moved and sedated. The patients begin to die again, at a death a day, and the nurses remember who signed for it.
