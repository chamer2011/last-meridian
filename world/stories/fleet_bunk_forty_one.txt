# The Man in Bunk Forty-One
kind: mystery
places: fleet
people: tarrow, halm, aduro, seven
factions: fleet
level: 3

The lead:
Aune Hollander shot her husband in their bunk on the Harrow's Bounty, then walked to the purser and reported it herself. She says it wasn't him, and half the ship is ready to carry her on their shoulders.

For anyone who isn't the captain:
Aune Hollander shot her husband in their bunk on the Harrow's Bounty, then walked to the purser and reported it herself. She says it wasn't him, and half the ship is ready to carry her on their shoulders.

What's really going on:
The fleet is terrified of mimics, and here's someone who acted. The autopsy is the first turn: he's flesh, all of him, no silver. So she's a murderer, and half the fleet doesn't care. The second turn comes to anyone who digs: he wasn't Joren Hollander either. Joren died in the Fall. This was Ned Vasek, a dock steward from Aurelis, who held Joren's hand at the end, promised to look after his family, took his ID and ration card in the chaos of the evacuation, and walked up to Aune and her two children wearing a dead man's name. Everyone was so shattered it almost worked. He has been a devoted father for six months. Aune half knew. His hands were wrong, and he didn't know the lullaby. The only explanation the fleet offers for that kind of wrongness is the Choir. So she's right, and wrong, and her daughter Mira, who's nine, wants to testify for him. And the fleet's registry was rebuilt from what people said. Security's best guess is that one person in thirty is living under a dead name.

How it might go:
- The brig on the Bounty, with a crowd outside bringing her food. Halm wants a civil court, Tarrow wants a military tribunal, and nobody has a judge. Everyone looks at the captain.
- The autopsy, by Aduro or Seven. Flesh. The crowd doesn't accept that, and if Seven did it they accept it even less.
- Something's wrong with the records: a dental scan, a dock pass, a dead man's fingerprints. Who was he?
- The trial, somewhere with benches. Mira wants to speak. Aune won't defend herself, and the prosecution isn't sure what to charge.
- The sentence. There's no prison in the fleet, no spare ration for one and no death penalty under Concord law, and the crowd has opinions on all three.

Ways through:
- Investigate: medicine and science on the body, awareness and hacking in the records, streetwise for someone from the Aurelis docks.
- Try the case: lore to set up a lawful court, persuasion to argue it, deception if the captain decides that what's known about Ned Vasek should stay unknown.
- Hold the crowd: leadership, intimidation and composure at the brig door, and a ruling they'll obey.
- Protect the others: tactics or stealth to keep the one-in-thirty figure out of this trial. If it gets out, Tarrow wants a census, and thousands of people go into hiding.

Where it can end up:
acquitted_by_crowd: Acquitted by the crowd: she goes free to cheers. The next frightened person has a precedent, and within a month somebody else is dead in a bunk.
convicted_with_sentence_fits: Convicted, with a sentence that fits: service on Anselm's Mercy, say, and raising the children. The fleet gets its first real judgment, and the captain has made law out of nothing.
name_comes_out: The name comes out: a census is demanded. People living under dead names, thousands of them, start to vanish into the lower decks. Some of them are on the captain's crew, and some are very good at their jobs.
airlock: The airlock: the crowd wins. Concord law dies on the Bounty's deck, the children watch, and Aune doesn't say a word. It doesn't have to be the end of the captain, but it's the end of something.
