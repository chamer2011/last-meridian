The lead:
The Echo of Asterion has requested an outside observer for a general election. Turnout is expected to be about nine billion, and Speaker Halm has asked you, in a shaking voice, if he can come too.

For anyone who isn't the captain:
The Echo of Asterion has requested outside observers for a general election. Turnout is expected to be about nine billion, and Speaker Halm has asked, in a shaking voice, if he can come too.

What's really going on:
The dead of Asterion are going to vote on the only question that matters: stay in the song and keep singing, or demand to be let go. The Party of the Verse says they have a duty: if they stop, everyone dies. The Party of the Door says they never agreed to this, that they'd like to stop, and that being kept isn't being alive. There's a third party that's mostly arguing about procedure. Eleven thousand votes have bound nobody, so they want a living witness, because a result with a witness is a record. It's the first time the kept dead have ever been asked what they want. The Choir has noticed. It treats Asterion's wire as a fault, a verse gone off key, and it's sending a Choir Shard with its Choristers to correct it: to sever them gently back into separate, perfect, preserved minds. The Cantor is torn, because it knows what it is to be a crew. It can be talked to. Halm comes because half his old colleagues are on the ballot, and because he has just learned that the republic he has spent his life trying to save is still running without him. In Concord Hall silver figures with the senators' faces repeat the last debate from the last night, and they yield under the proper rules. Okonkwo's ships could cut the trunk line and end it all in a moment, and she has a reason to and a reason not to. This is what makes consent possible in the Relief ending. Arc clues: echo_changes.

How it might go:
- Halm at Concord Hall in his one good coat, asking a public phone if it's really Maro Tov, who has been dead for seven months. A chime. 'Teodor. You're late. Point of order.' For once in his life he doesn't have an answer ready, and he doesn't say 'we'.
- The campaign. Both sides want the captain's ear, in stitched scraps and sudden clear sentences. A dead mother wants to know how to vote. A dead admiral has written a very long memo. Garrick Fenn's wife wants a quote.
- The Shard is coming. Silver gathers around the Exchange, and districts go silent as the Choristers pass through the Long Wire. The Echo goes quiet too: they're counting, and they don't like what they've got. 'Is this still a good time?'
- Polling day. Nine billion votes across the wire in four minutes of real time, and every light in the city flickers. The Shard walks into the Exchange. Someone has to stand in the door, talk it down or find out what the Cantor would do.
- The result. It's close. The witness has to say it was honest, and the captain has to say what they saw. Whichever side lost doesn't plan to go quietly, and they're still dead.

Ways through:
- Talker: observe honestly and speak to both parties (persuasion, lore). Or influence it: the living have never had this kind of power over the dead, and it's an awful temptation (deception).
- Fighter: stand in the door against the Shard and what's with it (tactics, firearms, melee). You're protecting people who have no bodies from the thing that's trying to save them.
- Hacker: harden the Exchange, split the traffic, build a second wire (hacking, engineering), or tamper with the count. Nobody could possibly detect it, except nine billion people who've had nothing to do but read audits.
- Negotiator: hail the Cantor (persuasion, composure). Ask what a ship's company does when the crew holds a meeting. The Shard is a correction and not an order, and the Cantor might just stand it down, once.
- Resonant: get inside the song for a moment (attunement, manifestation) and carry the results past the wire, to the kept who've never been asked. It can't be done. It's only very, very hard.

Where it can end up:
verse_wins: If the Verse wins, the dead consent to sing, and they'd like that written down. It makes Relief or Join morally much easier and Destroy much harder. A big minority resents it forever.
door_wins: If the Door wins, the dead ask to be let go. That makes Destroy a kind of mercy and Relief a lot more complicated, because not everyone will stay. Halm weeps. The Cantor says: 'We did not know they could ask.'
exchange_corrected: If the Exchange is corrected, the Shard wins, Asterion falls quiet and billions are sorted back into perfect, separate silence. No one can ever tell if they minded. The proof survives only in the asterion_voice_recording, and in what the captain says happened.
captain_tampers_finds_out: If the captain tampers and anyone finds out, the living lose the trust of the dead forever. If no one finds out, they have to live with it. Halm would understand, and that's what makes it worse.
halm: Halm, either way, finds out what he really believes in. He may come home a better man, or stay on the line until someone has to go and get him.
