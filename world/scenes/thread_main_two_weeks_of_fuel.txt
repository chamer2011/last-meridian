Quill's Parlor is a low warm room cut out of the rock and lined with cushions that somebody embroidered by hand, a great many years ago, in a great many colors. There's tea. There's a plate of something that's still steaming. In the corner, on a plain stool, a very quiet woman sits holding a long rifle across her knees the way other people hold a coat for a friend.

Mother Quill is small, and grey, and wrapped in a shawl held closed at the throat by a chip of pale carved stone, and she takes both your hands in both of hers before you've said a word. She asks after your mother, by name. You didn't tell her your mother's name.

And if your scholar ever finds anything the First Builders left lying about, I'd like to see it before anybody else does. That's the whole page. I worry about people who need more pages than that."

The page is on the low table between the teacups, one sheet, and her name is already on it.
