HOW TO WRITE A SCENE
====================

A scene is a plain text file in this folder, like opening_captain.txt. Open it in Notepad, change
it, save it, and the game uses the new version the next time the story reaches it. There's no need
to restart. To find mistakes, run this in the game's folder:

    npm run check-story

It lists every problem with the file and the line it's on. Nothing is changed by it. The tests
(npm test) check the scenes too.

Files whose names start with _ are notes like this one, and aren't scenes.


WHAT THE FILE'S NAME MEANS
--------------------------

opening_captain.txt    How the story opens for the captain. For another life, use its id from
                       starts.json: opening_engine_hand.txt, opening_docker.txt, and so on.
opening.txt            The opening for any life that has none of its own.
thread_<story>.txt     Plays when a story from the Leads menu is taken up, for example
                       thread_crew_naomi_under_crew.txt. The ids are the file names in world/threads.
arrive_<place>.txt     Plays when the player arrives at that place, by jump, passage or shuttle, when
                       they press Continue the story. The ids are the file names in world/places.
talk_<person>.txt      What that person says in a talk. See CONVERSATIONS below.


THE SHAPE OF A SCENE
--------------------

    # The title
    start: bridge

    == bridge
    Paragraphs of what the player reads. Lines run together until a blank line, which
    starts a new paragraph.

    @Commander Ilya Voss: "Someone speaking gets a line like this, with their name after @."

    => A line starting with => is a consequence, shown boxed, for something that will matter later.

    > A choice -> the_part_it_leads_to
    > Another choice -> somewhere_else

    == somewhere_else
    More text.
    END

Each part starts with == and a name made of letters, numbers and underscores. The first part is
where the scene starts, unless a start: line says otherwise. A part either has choices, or ends the
scene with END, or opens a check or a fight (see below), or has a rule that sends the story on.
Lines starting with // are comments, and the game ignores them.

Speech should be written exactly like the example, with the name after @ and the words in quotes.
That's how the words go in a window over that person's head when they're in the room. The game
knows the people in world/characters by their names and the names people call them.


CHOICES
-------

    > [+Voss] Ask her what she'd do -> voss_answer
    > [persuasion:moderate:voss] Ask what she isn't telling you -> voss_pressed_pass | voss_pressed_fail
    > [reason] Tell the fleet the truth -> truth
    > [! costs 200 credits] Pay him -> paid ? credits >= 200

Things in square brackets before the words are tags, which the player sees:

    [persuasion:moderate]        A skill check. The skill's id and the difficulty: easy, moderate,
                                 hard, very_hard or nearly_impossible. Add a person's id after a
                                 second colon when they're the one being persuaded, deceived or led,
                                 and how they feel about the player changes the odds. A choice with
                                 a check leads two ways: the part if it passes | the part if it fails.
    [+Voss] or [-Tarrow]         Someone who would clearly approve or disapprove.
    [reason]                     The belief the choice expresses: order, freedom, community, self,
                                 mercy, iron, reason, faith, purity or ascension.
    [! anything]                 A warning, such as a cost or a risk.

A choice can end with ? and a condition, and then it's only offered when the condition holds.
Conditions are explained below.


WHAT A PART CHANGES
-------------------

Lines starting with * happen when the part begins, and the player sees each as a game message:

    * journal: Words to write in the journal.
    * xp 10: The reason
    * credits -20: The reason               (a minus sign charges the player)
    * item +medkit                          (gives an item from world/items.json or src/data.js)
    * item -data_chip                       (takes one away)
    * quest the_beacon: The new objective   (changes a quest the player already has)
    * quest the_beacon done: How it ended   (or failed)
    * new quest find_the_scholar: The Title | What it's about | What to do first
    * contact Ilya Voss friendly: What happened between you     (hostile, wary, neutral, friendly or ally)
    * belief reason 1: The reason           (1 for a telling choice, 2 for a major one, 3 for a defining moment)
    * faction fleet 2: The reason           (a minus sign lowers it)
    * stress 5: The reason                  (a minus sign relieves it)
    * health -4: The reason                 (a minus sign hurts, and it can't kill)
    * time 2 hours: The reason              (add work or rest after hours if that's what it was; 72 at most in one part)
    * flag told_the_fleet                   (remembers something, for conditions later, in any scene)
    * flag kessara_closed: Kessara is closed to the fleet     (with words, the player reads it on the record,
                                            in the Quests menu, and so does the AI)
    * unflag told_the_fleet
    * ending sign_with_quill: How it ended   (ends this scene's story by one of the endings named in its file,
                                            world/stories: the quest is done with that line, and the ending's on
                                            the record. Put failed before the colon if the player lost it)
    * fuel 6: The reason                    (the fleet's fuel, which jumps use; a minus sign burns it)
    * act 2                                 (the main story moves on to that act)
    * raw add_lore {"term": "The Rim", "kind": "place", "text": "What the player would know."}

The last one does anything the AI can do, with the fields from src/tools.js. The rules are the
same as for the AI: there are limits on credits and XP in one turn, and a quest has to exist
before it can change.


CONDITIONS
----------

A condition goes after ? on a choice, or starts a rule line. Put "not" in front to turn one round,
and join several with "and":

    has data_chip                    The player carries that item.
    credits >= 20                    Also <=, >, <, = and !=, for credits, stress, health (as a
                                     percentage), day, and level.
    skill persuasion >= 3            A skill's rank. Also stat presence >= 4, faction fleet >= 5,
                                     and belief reason >= 2.
    flag told_the_fleet              A flag set with * flag.
    ended main_two_weeks_of_fuel     That story is over, any way it went.
    ended main_two_weeks_of_fuel raid_depot    It ended that way. The names are in its file, world/stories.
    seen voss_answer                 The player has been through that part in this scene.
    quest the_beacon active          Also done, failed, or none if they haven't got it.
    met Voss                         They're in the player's contacts.
    likes Voss                       Friendly or better. dislikes Voss is wary or worse.
    captain                          The player is the captain.
    life engine_hand                 The player started as that life. The ids are in starts.json.
    at haven                         The player is at that place.
    act < 2                          The main story's act, with the same comparisons as credits.
    chance 30                        True 30 times in 100, at random.

A condition with a mistake in it is never true, and check-story points it out.


RULES: GOING SOMEWHERE ELSE
---------------------------

A line starting with ? and a condition, then an arrow, sends the story to another part instead of
showing this one, when the condition holds. A plain -> is where to go otherwise. They're checked
in order, before the text:

    == plot
    ? flag told_the_fleet -> plot_after_truth
    ? credits < 10 -> plot_broke

    @Ensign Pell Asante: "Plotted, sir."
    END

A part that only has rules, and a final -> part, is a fork in the road with no text of its own.

The rules are read again when the player presses Continue the story while they're in that part,
so a part can wait for something. A part with ? at kessara -> parlor and text telling the player
to set course from the Star map sends the story on to the parlor once the fleet has jumped there
and they press Continue. The part's choices stay on offer meanwhile, for anything else they'd
rather do first.


A CHECK THE STORY DEMANDS
-------------------------

When the story itself calls for a check, rather than a choice:

    == hatch
    The hatch is locked, and there's no other way.
    CHECK engineering:moderate
      reason: Open the hatch
      pass -> open
      fail -> stuck

The indented lines belong to the CHECK. reason is what the player reads as the window opens.


A FIGHT
-------

    == ambush
    Three of them step out from behind the coolant tanks.
    FIGHT dock_thug
      lead_in: Nobody says anything. They don't need to.
      enemy_followers: dock_thug, drift_raider
      allies: voss
      why: paid
      reason: Somebody's paying them to break your legs.
      won -> won
      lost -> lost
      fled -> fled
      gaveUp -> gave_up
      talked -> talked_them_down

The enemy ids are the file names in world/enemies, and people are the file names in
world/characters. won and lost are needed, and the others are only if you want them: otherwise
running away or giving up counts as lost, and talking them down counts as won. why is why they're
fighting, which decides how hard they are to talk down: desperate, afraid, paid, greed, orders,
guarding, cause, belief, grudge or honor. The first line of text in a part with a FIGHT isn't
needed: the lead_in is what the player reads as it starts.


CONVERSATIONS
-------------

A file called talk_<id>.txt is what that person says when the player walks up to them, with the id
from world/characters: talk_voss.txt, talk_marrow.txt. It's written like a scene, and plays in the
talk window. The person speaks first, from the first part, and each choice is a thing the player can
say. A part with no choices is where the conversation runs out, and the player says goodbye.

    == start
    ? flag knows_about_the_list -> start_list
    @Commander Ilya Voss: "Captain. Go on."
    > What's our real situation? -> situation ? not seen situation
    > That's all for now -> done

    == situation
    @Commander Ilya Voss: "Fuel for two weeks."
    > That's all for now -> done

    == done
    @Commander Ilya Voss: "Captain."

In a conversation, "seen" means said in this conversation, so ? not seen situation hides a
question once it's been asked. Flags set anywhere last, so a conversation can know what the
story did. Effects (* lines) work, except time, travel and set_scene, because the clock stops
in a talk. A conversation can't open a CHECK or a FIGHT: put those in a scene.

Use ? not captain -> other at the start when the person would speak differently to someone who
isn't the captain. Anyone without a file of their own gets stock lines, made from who they are and
what they're doing, from the templates at the end of world/rooms.json. With an AI game master, the
AI plays them instead, and a person with a file still speaks from it.


THINGS AND DOORS
----------------

These are in the map files, world/maps/<place>.json, not here. A thing's used is what happens
when the player uses it without an AI (usedOther for someone who isn't the captain), and a thing
with an item is picked up. A closed door's ask is the rule when the player asks at it:

    "ask": { "check": "persuasion:moderate", "reason": "Talk your way past", "pass": "...", "fail": "..." }
    "ask": { "if": "flag quill_invited", "opens": "...", "else": "..." }
    "ask": { "opens": "...", "flag": "crossed_the_picket" }
    "ask": { "answer": "Not yet." }

if can be any condition from above. A door with if and check tries the condition first, then
the check. flag is remembered when the door opens.


GAME MESSAGES
-------------

    NOTE: [STORY] A message shown with the part, in the colored lines, not in the story's own voice.


NOTES FOR THE AI
----------------

    AI: Sable won't go below forty, and a threat makes her stop talking.

With an AI game master, the written parts still play as written, and clicking a choice is free.
But the player can also type their own words, and then the AI reads this part, these notes and
the choices, and either takes the choice the words come to, or answers in a sentence or two
without moving the story on. An AI: line is for the AI alone: the player never sees it. Use it
for what the people here would and wouldn't do, what they know, and what can't happen here, so
the AI's answers stay true to the part. Several AI: lines in one part are read together. Without
an AI the line does nothing. A conversation (talk_<id>.txt) can have them too.


WHAT THE PLAYER CAN TYPE
------------------------

Without an AI, the player picks from the choices, or types part of one. Typing is forgiving:
"say nothing" is enough for "Say nothing, and listen to it". Anything else is refused in plain
words, and nothing changes. So every part the player can stay in needs choices.

With an AI game master, anything else they type goes to the AI, which fits it to one of the
choices or answers it in a few words, and the written part still decides what happens next: the
AI can't change the game from a written part any other way. So the choices still have to cover
every way through, and an AI: line can say what the AI should and shouldn't allow in between.
