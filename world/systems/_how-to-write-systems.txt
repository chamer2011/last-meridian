HOW TO WRITE A PLACE'S SYSTEMS
==============================

Every place has its own systems: its power, its air, its lights, its doors, its cameras, its alarm,
its comms, and on a ship its engines and navigation, on a station its docking clamps. None of them
reaches another place. Every part of them is a real thing in a room: a junction box on the wall, a
vent in every room, a camera with a red light, a speaker, a relay. The player can walk up to any of
them and work it by hand, or run them from a computer that runs them.

A place's systems are one plain text file in this folder, world/systems/<place>.txt, named after the
place (the file name of its map in world/maps). A place without a file gets systems made for it from
its rooms and its things, the way _defaults.json says. The Story board shows any place's systems
under Systems, its own or the ones it gets by default, and saving one there writes its file here.

    npm run check-story

checks these files too.


THE FILE
--------

    # Haven Station
    draft: the AI's, not yet edited by the owner
    guards: Docker Renko, Docker Bo
    engineers: Air Tech Neve
    alarm rings: 30
    air goes bad: 20
    repairs take: 3

    Power:
    - junction box in The Manifest Docks for The Manifest Docks, The Hook
    - junction box in The Neutral Ground for The Neutral Ground, The Consulate Row: The inner breakers.

    Air:
    - air plant in Tharel's Warm Room
    - vent in every room

    Lights:
    - light switch in every room

    Doors:
    - door to The Consulate Row

    Cameras:
    - camera in The Manifest Docks, The Neutral Ground

    Alarm:
    - speaker in every room
    - locks the door to The Consulate Row

    Computers:
    - haven_harbor_office: everything
    - a watch console in The Neutral Ground: cameras, alarm, doors, class moderate

The first line is the place's name. Then, all of them can be left out:

    draft           Anything: the game doesn't read it. Say who wrote the file.
    guards          Who comes running when the alarm rings: people's ids (the file names in
                    world/characters), or the names of the people in the place's crowds in
                    world/life. none for nobody. Left out, it's whoever's day has guard, patrol,
                    security or sentry in it.
    engineers       Who fixes what's broken, in time. The same, and left out it's whoever's day has
                    engine, reactor, repair, pipes and the like in it. A place with no engineers
                    stays broken until the player fixes it.
    alarm rings     How many minutes the alarm rings before it stops by itself.
    air goes bad    How many minutes a room's air takes to go bad once it stops moving.
    repairs take    How many hours the engineers take to fix something.

The numbers every place starts from are in _defaults.json, with how long air takes to come back and
how long before the place's people switch back what the player switched off.


THE SYSTEMS
-----------

Each system is a heading with a colon, and a line for each of its parts, starting with - and a space:

    - the part in the room

The part is its name: junction box, vent, camera, or anything else. The room is the room's name, as
the map has it, or every room. Several rooms, separated by commas, mean one of the part in each.
After a colon, a line of your own says how the part looks:

    - camera in The Docks: A dome over the berth board, with a red light that never blinks.

The game knows what these do:

    Power        A junction box powers the room it's in, or the rooms after for. A room with no power
                 goes dark, and everything in it that needs power stops: its cameras, its vents, its
                 speakers, its screens, its door panels. A room no box powers always has power.
    Air          An air plant, and a vent in each room. When the plant stops, or a room's vent is
                 closed or sealed, or the room loses its power, the air there stops moving and goes
                 bad over minutes. People leave for a room with good air, though people asleep only
                 notice once it's bad. The player feels it: stress while it's thin, and harm once
                 it's bad, never down to nothing, and they can't rest in it. A place with no Air
                 has air anyway, like a planet with an atmosphere.
    Lights       A light switch in a room. A room with its lights off, or no power, is dark: drawn
                 dark, easier to sneak through, and harder to keep watch in.
    Doors        door to and a room: the ways into that room can be locked, from a computer, by
                 the alarm, or by the story. A locked door stops anyone walking through, both ways.
                 It can be forced, or its panel hacked. A door only the story opens (closed in the
                 map file) stays the story's, but a computer that runs it can open it.
    Cameras      A camera in a room watches it. Tampering with anything where a camera can see
                 raises the alarm. A camera on the player makes sneaking harder.
    Alarm        A speaker in a room, and locks the door to, which the alarm locks while it rings.
                 It rings where the player was seen, brings the guards to that room if comms are
                 up, costs standing with whoever holds the place, and puts the fact on the record
                 that the player was seen. It stops after a while, or from a computer.
    Comms        A relay. With every relay down, the guards don't hear the alarm.
    Engines      On the Meridian: with her engines down, the fleet can't jump.
    Navigation   On the Meridian: with it down, every jump is plotted by hand, a fuel more and half
                 as long again.
    Docking      Docking clamps: locked, the fleet can't leave the place.

Any other heading is a system of the place's own, like Water with a pump in it. It does nothing by
itself, but its parts are there to work, the story can ask about it and change it, and the AI hears
when it's down.

Each kind of part has a look, and its own work by hand, in _defaults.json: a junction box's lever and
its cables, a vent that can be sealed, a camera's lens that can be covered, a speaker's wires. A part
whose name isn't there behaves like the system's own part (a "scrubber" under Air like a vent, a
"generator" under Power like a junction box), or like a machine under a system of the place's own.

Parts go on the walls of their room, where the room can see them, and stand on the floor where a
room has no walls, always where they block nobody's way.


COMPUTERS
---------

Under Computers, each line is a computer and what it runs:

    - haven_harbor_office: everything
    - haven_berth_board: docking, cameras in The Manifest Docks
    - the plot table: navigation, doors
    - a watch console in The Neutral Ground: cameras, alarm, class moderate

The computer is one of the files in world/computers by its id, or a thing in one of the place's
rooms by its name (with in and the room if two rooms have one), or a new console the game puts in
the room you name, at the class you give it (easy, moderate, hard, very hard or nearly impossible).
After the colon, the systems by their headings, or everything, and in and rooms to run them only
there. A computer's screen gets a Systems list, with each part's state and buttons to switch it, and
on a hacked computer each switch costs a point of trace, like a program. A computer that cuts its
own room's power goes dark with it.

Without a file, every console, panel or terminal at a place runs what's in its own room, and a
server or a big computer runs everything.


IN THE WRITTEN STORY
--------------------

A condition can ask about them (world/scenes/_how-to-write-a-scene.txt has the rest):

    ? power off here                    ? lights off in The Docks
    ? air bad in The Hook               ? air thin, ? air good, ? air off, ? air on
    ? cameras off in The Docks          ? alarm ringing at haven, ? alarm quiet, ? alarm off
    ? door locked The Hook              ? comms jammed
    ? engines off at meridian           ? docking locked at haven
    ? system water off at frontier

and a part can change them:

    * power off in The Docks            * lights on in The Hook
    * air off                           (the air plant; * air off in a room closes its vent)
    * cameras off in The Docks          * alarm ringing in The Docks, * alarm quiet, * alarm off
    * door locked The Hook              * door unlocked The Hook
    * comms jammed                      * engines off, * navigation off, * docking locked
    * system water off at frontier
    * fix everything                    * fix power in The Docks
    * break engines at meridian

What the story changes stays changed until the story changes it back. What the player switches off
at someone else's place is switched back by its people after a while, and what they break is fixed
by its engineers.
